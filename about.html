About

I'm Tsolmondorj Natsagdorj, a systems and research engineer. I work across reliable AI, high-performance networking, scientific ML, autonomous systems, and security-critical infrastructure.

The through-line is correctness under hidden failure modes: benchmarks that leak their labels, mocks that agree with the bug they are testing, uncertainty estimates that do not track error, protocol invariants that prove less than their diagrams imply, and ambitious hardware that needs physical gates before performance claims.

Formation explains how protocol invariants, evaluation validity, calibration, hardware boundaries, and physical autonomy became one engineering trajectory rather than five unrelated interests.

Now

I build Suwappu and related systems, work on BRIDGE-bench and AI-evaluation methodology, maintain scientific-ML experiments around materials discovery, and prototype Aiur, an autonomous airborne-carrier research program. I also contribute correctness fixes and infrastructure upstream when a problem belongs in the shared library rather than only my project.

Research practice

I separate observed, derived, estimated, and target claims. A later validity failure is allowed to weaken an earlier result. That is why the materials and BRIDGE-bench articles carry visible revisions instead of preserving the flattering interpretation.
