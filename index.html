I'm Tsolmondorj Natsagdorj. I build systems that have to survive contact with reality: reliable AI, high-performance networking, scientific ML, autonomous systems, and security-critical infrastructure.

The recurring question in my work is simple: what evidence would reveal that the abstraction is lying?

Now

  • AI evaluations — separate capability from dataset metadata.

    BRIDGE-bench's historical score is now treated as contaminated evidence after an audit found severe author-injected leakage in 13 of 24 verified prompts. The current work is measurement design, sanitizer invariants, and matched controls—not inventing a rerun result.

  • Autonomy — make recovery boring before making the carrier large.

    Aiur CARRIER-P0 deletes hydrogen, swarms, charging, outdoor autonomy, and airborne compute until one risky interaction remains: mechanically verified recovery of one micro-UAV.

  • Open source — correctness at somebody else's boundary.

    Recent merged work spans MatGL uncertainty/autograd, MLX-LM sampling defaults, Alloy EIP-712 recursion, and uutils date/time compatibility.

BRIDGE-bench started as an evaluation of model reasoning over real exploit contracts. Its most important result was a benchmark-validity failure: 13/24 prompts contained severe provenance leakage, including one exact-label case whose vulnerable function was absent from source.
